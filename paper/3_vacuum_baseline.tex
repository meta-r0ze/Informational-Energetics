\section{Defining The Vacuum Baseline}
\label{sec:the_test_case}
The six pillars are proven necessary for any persistent system by counterfactual analysis. The vacuum is the \emph{maximally constrained} case: it has no external flux, memory, or setpoint to assist persistence. Open systems may supplement specific pillar functions from their environment (for
example, offsetting dissipation through external energy flux rather than minimizing $S_\Phi$ directly), but they cannot eliminate the architectural requirement. What is necessary without assistance is
a fortiori necessary with assistance; the vacuum derivation therefore establishes the lower bound for all persistent systems.

We define the quantum vacuum as a persistent system in a state of Quiescent Equilibrium, operating with zero external flux and strictly subject to the six IE pillars. This establishes an absolute boundary condition. The vacuum admits no deeper system beneath it. It cannot obtain configuration information, boundary conditions, synchronization signals, or memory from an external source. We formalize this closed-system condition as:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{The Closure Constraint:} The substrate of the vacuum must be entirely self-defining. Its geometry must arise exclusively from its own architectural requirements.
\end{quote}
Any non-zero selection entropy would require an external memory to resolve the degeneracy, violating self-specification.
\label{subsec:closure}

This paper tests whether IE mapped to the vacuum derives a unique substrate, and whether that substrate's geometric properties match experimental expectations.
