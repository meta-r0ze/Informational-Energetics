\section{Defining The Vacuum Baseline}
\label{sec:the_test_case}
The vacuum is the persistent substrate from which all observable structures emerge. Particles are transient excitations; they have lifetimes and decay. The vacuum state itself persists. We define the quantum vacuum as a persistent system in a state of Quiescent Equilibrium, operating with zero external flux and strictly subject to the six IE pillars. This establishes an absolute boundary condition. The vacuum admits no deeper system beneath it. It cannot obtain configuration information, boundary conditions, synchronization signals, or memory from an external source. We formalize this closed-system condition as:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{The Closure Constraint:} The substrate of the vacuum must be entirely self-defining. Its geometry must arise exclusively from its own architectural requirements.
\end{quote}
Any non-zero selection entropy would require an external memory to resolve the degeneracy, violating self-specification.
\label{subsec:closure}

This paper tests the foundational claim: that persistence requirements alone constrain the vacuum's structure. We derive only the static geometric baseline (the lattice, its invariants, and the dimensionless impedance $Z_{\mathrm{geo}}$) that serves as the infrared fixed point of the continuum dual. If this baseline fails to match experiment, there is no framework to extend. If it holds, the continuous Lagrangian, equations of motion, renormalization-group flow, and gauge structure become the natural next layer. We defer them not because they are out of reach, but because they are unnecessary to falsify the core claim.
